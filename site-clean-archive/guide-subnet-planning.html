Subnet Planning

Every office network needs a subnet plan before it grows. Get it wrong and you will be fighting for IPs, or worse — you will run out.

The four numbers that matter

  • Mask / prefix — how many bits are "network". /24 means 24 network bits, 8 host bits.
  • Network address — the first address in the range. Not usable by a device.
  • Usable hosts — devices you can actually put on the wire. For a /24 that is 254.
  • Broadcast — the last address. Not usable by a device.

How many IPs do you actually need?

Count the real devices per segment: laptops, phones, printers, cameras, IoT, plus headroom. Rule of thumb:

  • Under 50 devices → /26 (62 usable)
  • 50–100 → /25 (126 usable)
  • 100–250 → /24 (254 usable)
  • More than that → split into /23s or smaller and route between them
Common mistake: giving one floor a /24 because "it is standard", then discovering the printer VLAN needs 200 IPs and the office only has 40 devices. Match the prefix to the count, not the habit.

Reading the calculator output

Run your CIDR through the Subnet Calculator. What to check:

  • Network and broadcast — if either one is inside your planned device range, your plan overlaps. Redo it.
  • Usable hosts — if this is less than your device count, bump up one prefix size.
  • Range — confirm it does not collide with an existing VLAN. Collisions do not error out; they just silently break traffic.
Overlap is the dangerous one. Two subnets on one flat network that share addresses = every device in that overlap range picks the wrong one. The calculator shows you the exact range — check it against your IP register before you push it.
