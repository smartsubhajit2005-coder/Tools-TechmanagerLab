Check If Your Email Is Spoofable

Most businesses have email that can be used by strangers to impersonate you. Before you accept an inbox, run a quick SPF/DKIM/DMARC lookup.

What the records mean

  • SPF v=spf1 — lists the IPs that are allowed to send mail for the domain.
  • DKIM default._domainkey — shows a public key that signed mail originates.
  • DMARC _dmarc — gives a policy about how to treat failed messages.

Using the lookup tool

Navigate to SPF /DKIM /DMARC Lookup and enter the domain you want to check. Scores that missing points for:

  • SPF v=spf1 ~all (soft fail) instead of v=spf1 -all (hard fail) when untrusted IPs can appear.
  • No DKIM key at all – many small providers forget to publish it.
  • DMARC set to none instead of reject or quarantine.
Common audit failure: a domain that allows * in SPF. That lets anyone send you mail as you – phishing, spoofing, brand theft.

After you’ve fixed the record, run the lookup again to confirm changing from public anyone to the real mail source.
